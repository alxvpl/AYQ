The engine itself is linear and fast: 250 000 transactions analyse in 39 ms, a comparison in 82 ms, and every headline agrees with the naive sum exactly. Validation is not linear: 50 000 → 382 ms, 100 000 → 1 264 ms, 250 000 → 6 843 ms (2.5× the input, 5.4× the time). The cause is in the contract validator, `ayq/ayq-analytical-contract/src/validate.ts` line 722: `const index = transactionOrder.indexOf(transaction)` inside the loop over every transaction — O(n²) whether or not the transaction is a reversal or a transfer. Validation runs at every launch and at every load, so at 250 000 transactions the application spends 6.8 s of its 9 s to first result there. **Not changed**: it is a performance characteristic of the shared contract package, not a breach of a rule; the correction (carry the index from the loop, or `entries()`) is one line and returns to the joint leads because the package is shared with the AYQ producer.

The validator still refuses what it must at 50 000 transactions (each in ~500 ms): a duplicate transactionKey, a reversal of a missing original, `meta.counts.transactions` wrong by one, a full IBAN in evidence text, an unsafe amount, an unresolved counterparty reference — refused as invariant / invariant / invariant / unknown / malformed / invariant.

The cause is the chart's height: `src/ui/result.tsx` sizes the chart host at `rows × 44 + 48` px with no upper bound, and ECharts allocates a canvas of that size at the device pixel ratio. Measured canvases: S2 (161 rows) 2 136 × 12 481 px; S3 (540 rows) 2 136 × 41 664 px, painted; **S5 (1 377 rows) 2 136 × 106 113 px, not painted** — `getImageData` finds no painted pixel where the bars should be, the chart area is blank, and the application says nothing. The table beside it is complete and correct. At 175 % the chart goes blank somewhere above ≈ 850 rows (canvas height > 65 535 px); at 100 % the same happens above ≈ 1 490 rows. Below that point the cost is memory: a 540-row chart holds a 41 664-px canvas per zrender layer in the renderer and its textures in the GPU process, which is where the 4–17 GB comes from. S5 is cheaper than S3 precisely because its canvas is never rasterised.

**Not changed**: every remedy — a bounded chart height, a scrolling chart, a top-N with the rest in the table, virtualised bars — is a chart-language decision under r003 §13, and the table is the surface r003 §8 governs. Returned to the joint leads with these numbers. 037's Observation A (labels cut mid-word without a mark) is present on every shape with long names and is likewise chart-language.

Where it becomes unpleasant, on this machine: first result under 1.3 s to 10 000 transactions and under 3.3 s to 100 000; validation alone crosses 1 s at ≈ 90 000 transactions and dominates from there; redraw on a context change stays under 200 ms up to ≈ 160 rows, 300–500 ms at 400–700 rows; memory becomes the problem before time does — from ≈ 300 rows on screen the GPU process passes 2 GB, and a machine with 8 GB would not survive S3's 13 GB.

No boundary of r004 §8.10 or 02_ARCHITECTURE §7 was crossed at any scale: the renderer received only the validated snapshot and the display name, over the four channels, and no path. One observation of the same kind: the installed application accepts Chromium's `--remote-debugging-port` switch (this is how it was measured), which gives whoever launches it that way full DevTools access to the renderer and the snapshot in memory — local, at the launching user's own trust level, and not a crossing of the renderer boundary, but worth knowing.

## W3 — Observation B, traced

`scale/trace-scrollbars.mjs` asks the real application, through the DevTools Protocol, which elements scroll vertically (`overflow-y` auto/scroll with `scrollHeight > clientHeight`, plus the document itself), with the viewport emulated at 1360 × 860, 1100 × 720 and 1920 × 1080, for Last month and This year, in three states: the bare result, the detail pane open, the coverage flyout open.

| state | result |
|---|---|
| bare result, any size, 6–308 rows | exactly one scroller, `main.body` (`overflow-y: auto`), with its 15 px bar; the document does not scroll |
| detail pane open | the same one scroller |
| coverage flyout open, 2–12 accounts | the same one scroller |
| coverage flyout open, 16 accounts | at 1100 × 720: **two** — `main.body` and `html` (document 847 px in a 720 px viewport) |
| coverage flyout open, 20 accounts | at 1360 × 860 and 1100 × 720: two (document 999 / 1 016 px) |
| coverage flyout open, 24 accounts | at every size including 1920 × 1080: two (document 1 107–1 176 px) |
| coverage flyout open, 50 accounts | two at every size (document 2 147–2 440 px) |

So the second bar is the **window's own**: the coverage flyout is a Fluent `Popover` rendered in a portal outside `main.body`, one line per account for coverage and one for reconciliation (~40 px per account), and it is never clamped to the viewport. When it is taller than the remaining height it overflows the document, `html` (overflow `visible`) becomes a scroller, and the window's bar appears beside `main.body`'s on the same right edge — exactly 037's picture. The account count at which it starts: ≈ 16 at 720 px, ≈ 20 at 860 px, ≈ 24 at 1080 px of viewport height, sooner with account names that wrap. Nothing else produced a second scroller at any size or result length tested. Whether the owner's capture had the flyout open, and how many accounts his snapshot holds, is not inferred here.

**Not changed.** The shell is r003 §3; a maximum height with the flyout scrolling inside itself, or a different container, is the joint leads' to accept.

## W1 — what the conformance suite enforces

`test/conformance.test.ts` (part of `npm test`): the 61 normative sentences of r003 §§3.1, 6.2, 6.3, 6.5, 6.6, 7, 8.1, 9, 11.4, 11.5, 11.6 in the catalogue character for character, each referenced from the module that owns it and, for the mapping modules, that module imported and used by the renderer; the three unknown-start sentences selected by the number of accounts; nothing accompanying a second launch in the main process or the catalogue; the status bar rendering only its two keys and naming no file, path or identifier; Settings reading only `identity.fileName`, `meta.generatedAt`, `accounts.length` and `transactions.length` from the snapshot; no internal identifier, state name, reason code, registry token, snake_case or camelCase word in any catalogue text; a reason code reaching the screen only through the catalogue mapping; and the contrast rules of r003 §4 as a table of every accent- or state-coloured foreground against every surface it is drawn on, after every overlay and wash, the lowest governing — including Fluent's own rest, hover and pressed surfaces for the outline button and the dialog surface, read from the theme.

One finding from the rules, reported rather than asserted away: the attention marker (`· limited`, `· start unknown`) inside the coverage button sits, while the button is pressed, on Fluent's `colorNeutralBackground1Pressed` (`#e0e0e0`) and measures **4.15:1** — below the 4.5:1 of r003 §4 for that transient state (rest 5.47:1, hover 5.02:1). The suite keeps that measurement as a `todo` test that reports its failure on every run without failing the suite, until the joint leads decide the pressed surface, the tone, or the rule for a transient state.

What the suite cannot honestly enforce: "rendered" reachability (no DOM in the suite — static reachability only; the installed-application evidence carries the screen); contrast from sampled pixels of a running window (measured from the token values and the composited surfaces the stylesheet and theme define); chart labels and tooltips (outside r003 by §13, drawn by ECharts).
